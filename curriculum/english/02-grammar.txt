The verb changes after he, she, or it in the simple present tense.

I am reading now.
She is reading now.

The present continuous describes an action in progress.

I walked home yesterday.
She found the missing page.
They completed the task before lunch.
The simple past describes a completed event.

I will call tomorrow.
She will bring the map.
They will arrive before noon.
The word will can describe a future event.

The device was tested by the engineer.
The active sentence emphasizes the person acting.
The passive sentence emphasizes the thing affected.

The green light is bright.
The small green light is very bright.
Adjectives describe nouns, and adverbs can describe adjectives or verbs.

The reader examined the sentence carefully.

Carefully modifies the verb examined.

Conjunctions connect words, phrases, and clauses.

If the switch is on, the lamp glows.
If the switch were on, the lamp would glow.
If the switch had been on, the lamp would have glowed.
Conditional sentences distinguish facts, possibilities, and unreal past events.

The student who asked the question received an explanation.
The tool that measures temperature is on the desk.
The office where we met is now closed.
Relative clauses add information about a noun.

Maya gave the report to Leon.
She gave it to him.
Pronouns replace nouns when their reference is clear.
An unclear pronoun can make a sentence ambiguous.

The old bridge needs repair.
The engineers inspected it last week.
They found damage near the eastern support.

Use a comma to separate items in a list.
Use a colon to introduce an explanation or a list.
Use quotation marks around direct speech.
Use an apostrophe in a contraction or to show possession.

The result is accurate.
The result is not accurate.
Is the result accurate?
How accurate is the result?
Word order helps distinguish statements from questions.
